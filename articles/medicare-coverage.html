Does Medicare Cover Compression Garments for Lymphedema?

October 16, 2026 · 5 min read · Written by Missy Garner, Owner, P&H Services.

Yes. Since January 1, 2024, Medicare Part B covers compression garments and related supplies for people with lymphedema, whether it followed cancer treatment or surgery, or you've had it since you were young. If you were ever told Medicare didn't cover these, that used to be true, which is exactly why a lot of people are still paying out of pocket or going without. P&H fits and supplies these garments, and we verify your benefits to determine coverage and any out-of-pocket expenses.

The short version

  • Covered: yes, since January 1, 2024
  • What's covered: daytime and nighttime garments (ready-made and custom), wraps, bandaging, accessories
  • What you need: a lymphedema diagnosis from your doctor, PA, or NP in your visitation notes, and a written order from your provider (a doctor or a physical therapist) that includes the affected area(s), laterality (left, right, or bilateral), specific garment recommendations, the desired compression level, whether it is custom or off-the-shelf, and that the garment is recommended to manage your lymphedema
  • What you pay: with Traditional Medicare, 20% after your Part B deductible

Yes, and what changed

For years, Medicare had no benefit category for lymphedema compression garments, so people paid for them out of pocket or simply went without. That changed with the Lymphedema Treatment Act, which took effect on January 1, 2024 and created Medicare's first dedicated coverage for these supplies (Medicare.gov). For a lot of people, the old answer stuck. They asked once, heard no, and never asked again. If that's you, this is worth a second look, because the benefit is real and current.

What Medicare covers

Under Part B, coverage includes:

  • Daytime compression garments, both ready-made and custom
  • Nighttime compression garments
  • Gradient compression wraps with adjustable straps
  • Compression bandaging supplies
  • Accessories like donning aids, lymph pads, comfort linings, and similar items

It applies to any part of the body affected by lymphedema, and it covers ready-made garments as well as custom, not one or the other (Medicare.gov, Noridian). Daytime and nighttime garments do different jobs, and Medicare covers both, so you're not forced to choose.

How often you can get new garments

Traditional Medicare allows up to 3 daytime garments or wraps per affected limb every 6 months, and up to 2 nighttime garments per affected limb every 2 years. That schedule exists because garments lose their stretch and their pressure over months of daily wear and washing, so they need replacing on a continual basis.

What you need to qualify

Two things: 1) Practitioner Visit Note: A physician, NP, or PA visit note documenting the lymphedema diagnosis, along with specific garment recommendations. The recommendations can come from a physical therapist's note, provided the physician, NP, or PA visit note documents the diagnosis. 2) A written order or prescription listing the specific garments being ordered.

The order and documentation must include the affected area(s) and laterality (left, right, or bilateral), specific garment recommendations, the desired compression level, whether the garment is custom or off-the-shelf, and that the garment is recommended to manage your lymphedema. If it's custom, it also has to say why a custom garment is required. The order can come from a doctor or a therapist.

Two examples of documentation that works well:

  • Off-the-shelf: “Patient requires a left, thigh-high, 30-40 mmHg daytime compression garment to manage left, lower-extremity lymphedema.”
  • Custom: “Patient requires custom, bilateral, below-knee, 20-30 mmHg compression garments to manage bilateral, lower extremity lymphedema. Custom garments are required because the patient’s limb dimensions fall outside available ready-made sizing.”

If this feels like a lot, we can point you to the Medicare documentation-requirements page, and we check that the order has what it needs before anything goes to your plan. [Link: Medicare documentation requirements]

What you'll pay

With Traditional Medicare, you're responsible for 20% of the cost after your Part B deductible, and there's no prior authorization for Traditional Medicare (Lymphedema Advocacy Group). What you actually pay depends on the garment(s) prescribed. We verify your benefits to determine your coverage and any out-of-pocket expenses before placing any orders.

If you have Medicare Advantage

Medicare Advantage plans have to cover what Traditional Medicare covers, so the benefit is there either way. What can differ is the details: the quantities, which suppliers are in-network, and whether the plan wants prior authorization first (Lymphedema Advocacy Group). If you're on an Advantage plan, those are the things to check. Give us your plan information and we'll verify it for you.

What this benefit does not cover

This coverage is specifically for lymphedema. Compression prescribed for a different reason on its own, like venous insufficiency without lymphedema, is handled under different rules and may not fall under this benefit (Noridian). That distinction matters, because a claim billed the wrong way gets denied. If you're not sure which situation applies to you, we can sort that out when we verify your benefits, so you know where you stand before anything is ordered.

For referring providers: referral vs. order

If you're referring a patient, here's the part worth getting right. The lymphedema diagnosis must come from a doctor (any doctor the patient sees), a PA, or an NP, not a therapist. The order and supporting documentation can come from a doctor or a therapist. To keep the claim clean and avoid the most common denials:

  • Make sure there's a valid lymphedema diagnosis on file
  • Stay within the frequency limits above
  • Don't bill venous insufficiency under the lymphedema benefit, since that falls outside it (Noridian)

FAQ

Does Medicare cover compression garments for lymphedema?
Yes, since January 1, 2024, under the Lymphedema Treatment Act, for people with a lymphedema diagnosis and an order from their provider.
Do I need to see a specialist?
No specialist required. The diagnosis can come from any doctor you see, a PA, or an NP, and the order can come from a doctor or a therapist.
How often can I get new garments?
Up to 3 daytime garments per limb every 6 months, and 2 nighttime per limb every 2 years.
What will I pay?
With Traditional Medicare, 20% after your Part B deductible. Your exact cost depends on the garment(s) prescribed, and we verify your benefits to determine your coverage and any out-of-pocket expenses.
Does Medicare Advantage cover it?
Yes. Advantage plans must cover what Traditional Medicare covers, though quantities and in-network suppliers can vary by plan.
